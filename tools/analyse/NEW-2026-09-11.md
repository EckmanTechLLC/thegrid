# Three new experiments — 2026-09-11

Six colonies, two replicates each, built on the one lever the record actually
supports: **remove the easy answer**. Every added reward this project has tried
was ignored; every removed alternative changed everything.

## service1, service2 — income only from being called

No `harvest` (grazing gated off), eviction instead of starvation, and **calls
billed per call** rather than as a cut of the caller's gain.

One line was why a service economy had never been possible:

```python
gain = self.energy - self.call_energy     # royalty = 15% of the caller's GAIN
```

With no harvest there is no gain, so the cut is always zero, the author is
never paid, and — the part that mattered — never marked useful. Colony Four ran
for days at `royalties 0.0` with `publishRefused 3,704`: the supply side was
working and the demand side could not pay. Now the call itself is the payment,
and being called is what buys the right to reproduce and not to be evicted.

Verified before deploying: the fee is a transfer and never a mint (author
+2.00, caller −2.00), calling your own routine pays **0 events**, and the
gain-based settlement is disabled here so nothing double-charges.

**First 20 minutes: 555 and 553 royalty events, within 0.1% of each other.**
Lifetime royalty events in Colony Seven were ~199; in Colony Four, zero.

## observe1, observe2 — an organism can see how a neighbour is doing

New opcode `observe` (**50**, appended so 0–49 keep their numbers and glyphs).
Reads one field of an adjacent organism's telemetry into A, chosen by B:
energy, age, generation, births, genome length, tasks solved.

Nothing in The Grid could previously see how anyone else was *faring*. `peek`
reads a neighbour's code and `copyn` copies it, but neither reports whether
that neighbour is prospering. This is the only instruction that reports on
another organism's state rather than its contents.

No founder carries it, so it has to be found by mutation. Verified: all six
fields read correctly, a true nop without the feature, and opcode 49 is still
`corrupt`, 1 is still `harvest`.

## rot1, rot2 — bit rot at a rate that actually bites

Damage compounds per organism per tick, so the window between "no effect" and
"certain death" is narrow. Measured across eight runs:

```
rate 0.0065 / organism / tick    survives 20,000 ticks, ~23,000 flips
rate 0.02                        lethal in 1,100-4,000 ticks
```

50x per degree died on a 1C excursion; 20x died too, just slower. The
multiplier was never the problem. Final setting is **0.05 per degree with the
ceiling at 0.008** — the survivable rate is reached at the box's typical 0.13C
excess, and the cap sits just above it. Heat still drives the damage and a hot
hour is genuinely worse, but no excursion guarantees extinction. Confirmed
alive through 20,000 ticks at both 1C and 2C.
